    How the conversion works

    • SVG drawings become real ES3D shapes. Path data is kept as path data, so curves stay curves. Filled shapes are extruded. Strokes become round tubes. Colours become materials. Later shapes sit on top of earlier ones. Text, images, filters, masks and scripts are skipped (convert text to paths in your drawing program first). Gradients become their average colour. Script in the SVG is not run.
    • OBJ, STL and glTF models become ES3D <mesh> elements, with their materials and, for glTF, their reuse of meshes and their transforms. That is the escape hatch for content that has no procedural description. The file is about as large as the original. Textures, skins and animations are not converted.
    • STEP solids from CAD software become real ES3D shapes. A part that is one shape is written as one: a box, an extrusion of its exact outline (with arcs, ellipses, splines and holes), a cylinder, a sphere, a torus, or a revolve of its profile for a turned part. Any other part is written face by face: flat faces become paths with exact arcs and holes, and fillets, hole walls and rounded corners become revolves through the angle they cover. ES3D has no free-form (NURBS) surfaces, so a free-form face, or a curved face cut along a curve that is not one of its circles (the rim of a cross hole), is tessellated from its exact surface into a mesh, and the note says how many. Coordinates stay in the file's unit, and a Z-up file is turned Y-up by a rotation on its outer group. Tessellated STEP stays a mesh.
    • ES3D documents convert to glTF (with instancing), OBJ, STL and tessellated STEP at the level of detail you choose, so you can take them into a 3D program, a game engine or a slicer. They also convert between the ES3D encodings (minified and compact). Those encodings are the same document, not a reduced profile.

    The same converter is in the command-line tool: npx @es3d/es3d import drawing.svg -o drawing.es3d. See the download page.
